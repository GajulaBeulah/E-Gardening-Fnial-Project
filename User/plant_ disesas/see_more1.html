Tomato Plant Disease Identification

Learn symptoms, causes, and treatment methods for tomato plant diseases.

1. Tomato Leaf Spot

Plant Identification

Tomato leaf spot is a fungal disease affecting tomato leaves. It creates dark circular spots with yellow borders.

Symptoms

  • Brown and black spots on leaves
  • Yellow patches around infected areas
  • Leaves dry and fall early
  • Reduced fruit quality and growth

How to Take Care

  • Remove infected leaves immediately
  • Avoid watering leaves directly
  • Use fungicide spray weekly
  • Keep proper spacing between plants

2. Early Blight

Plant Identification

Early blight is a common fungal disease caused by Alternaria fungus. It mainly attacks older tomato leaves first.

Symptoms

  • Dark brown rings on leaves
  • Yellowing around spots
  • Leaves dry from bottom upward
  • Weak plant growth

How to Take Care

  • Use disease-free seeds
  • Apply organic fungicide
  • Remove dead plant material
  • Rotate crops every season

3. Late Blight

Plant Identification

Late blight spreads quickly during cool and wet weather. It can destroy tomato plants within days.

Symptoms

  • Large dark water-soaked spots
  • White fungal growth under leaves
  • Stem rot and fruit decay
  • Sudden plant collapse

How to Take Care

  • Remove infected plants quickly
  • Improve air circulation
  • Avoid overhead watering
  • Use copper fungicide

4. Septoria Leaf Spot

Plant Identification

Septoria leaf spot is caused by fungal infection and commonly appears during humid conditions.

Symptoms

  • Small gray circular spots
  • Black dots inside spots
  • Leaves turn yellow
  • Heavy leaf dropping

How to Take Care

  • Prune affected leaves
  • Use clean gardening tools
  • Mulch around the plant
  • Apply fungicide treatment

5. Bacterial Speck

Plant Identification

Bacterial speck affects leaves and tomato fruits with tiny dark lesions. It spreads through contaminated water and tools.

Symptoms

  • Small black spots on leaves
  • Dark specks on fruits
  • Yellow halos around spots
  • Slow plant growth

How to Take Care

  • Use certified healthy seeds
  • Disinfect gardening equipment
  • Avoid touching wet plants
  • Spray copper-based bactericide
